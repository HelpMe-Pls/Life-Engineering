- **Know which lane you're in (Researcher-verified, and it's your best stat):** the ENGINEER-visa segment is *growing* — **108,334 Vietnamese held it at end-2024, +~20% YoY, now the #2 residence status** — while the yen-hammered technical-intern (thực tập sinh) lane softens. You sell to the white-collar lane. Say so in the marketing: "dành cho kỹ sư có bằng đại học" — it flatters the buyer AND filters the audience. (The 2027 Ikusei Shūrō reform targets the blue-collar feeder lane at CEFR A1 — it doesn't threaten this niche, but it will flood the low-end conversation with noise; position explicitly ABOVE that tier.)
- **The gap we own — corrected to a BUNDLE wedge, not a first-mover wedge (Researcher):** general JLPT prep is saturated and cheap (Dũng Mori/Riki, 1.5–2.4M₫/level; Dũng Mori even has a FREE construction-vocab flashcard module). And a near-competitor exists: **Zenith Pathways sells engineer interview-prep + Japanese-CV modules at 1.2–2.4M₫ per 4-session block.** What nobody bundles: **technical construction Japanese + 面接 mock interviews + dossier + retake accountability in ONE cohort with placement-outcome framing.** The bundle and the construction-technical depth are the wedge. Verticalize; never compete on general JLPT; expect prospects to compare you to Zenith's per-module pricing.
- **The economic argument that closes sales — sharpened by the council:** dispatch (phái cử) fees fall with Japanese level (≈ $4,500 no-Japanese → $3,000 N3 → $0–2,000 N2, corroborated) — AND top firms like Esuhai/JVNET already run **$0-fee engineer dispatch for strong candidates**. So the pitch is NOT "we save you the fee" (a firm can waive it) — it's **"we make you the candidate who QUALIFIES for the $0-fee, 35–70M₫/mo orders instead of the paid, lower-tier ones — independent of any single company."** Independence is load-bearing: the Contrarian's finding is that dispatch firms bundle free training *conditional on signing with them* (they're paid on placement); your counter-position is the **trusted independent guide** — prep that belongs to the candidate, not the recruiter, plus honest guidance on which đơn hàng/firms won't screw them. That trust layer is unbundleable.
- **Risks (design around, don't deny):**
  - *Weak yen narrows the wage gap* (JP foreign construction wages ~¥200K/mo stagnant; VN wages rising). Demand is FX-sensitive. **Hedge:** position the curriculum as "engineer-abroad" with Japan as the first destination; keep Korea/Germany modules as future options; sell the *transformation* (international career), not the country.
  - *Regulatory shift:* TITP → Ikusei Shūrō (ESD) on 2027-04-01 makes pre-arrival Japanese **mandatory and standardized** — net tailwind for language e-learning, but watch who pays (candidates vs sending orgs) as rules land.
  - *Single-instructor dependency:* one person's face and health carry the brand. Mitigate later with recorded depth + community; accept it now.

## 8.5 The one-man-machine operating manual (Khoi's side of the two-man show — scale without burning out)
This venture has two humans but only ONE operator of everything invisible. The instructor teaches, closes, and is the face; you are the machine. The machine survives 6+ months on evenings only if these rules hold:

- **What this venture does NOT need from you (read for relief):** no realtime support (the product is video + scheduled lives, not an app people are blocked inside at 2am), no daily content from you (batch model — the instructor's face, your editing), no pager (checkout is event-sourced and idempotent; a missed webhook retries — worst case a student waits an hour for access, not a compliance fine). Nothing here justifies a 1am wake-up.
- **The role firewall (the #1 burnout vector is leakage, not volume):** every pedagogical question — grammar, homework, motivation — routes to the instructor, ALWAYS. You never become the tutor. Your entire student-facing surface is: receipts, access problems, payment issues — answered from ONE fixed 30-minute window per day, nothing outside it. The mirror rule protects him too: he never touches platform config, deploys, or the funnel sheet. Role leakage in either direction is worth raising at the weekly sync the week it appears, not month 3.
- **Batch or it doesn't happen:** one weekend block = the week's editing batch (CapCut template ×N — after the template exists, a short is minutes, not an hour); scheduled posting via each platform's native scheduler; the multi-GB ingest pipeline (step 0.5) runs overnight unattended — you queue it, sleep, verify the digest in the morning. Ad-hoc "just one quick edit" requests accumulate to the weekend batch, never to your weeknights.
- **One sync, not a chat stream:** a fixed 30-minute weekly call with the instructor (same slot every week) carries ALL coordination — content calendar, funnel numbers, student patterns, next-cohort decisions. Zalo pings between syncs are for genuinely-blocking items only. (Same "questions accumulate to the call" mechanic as the sibling runbook's design partners — it works on partners too.)
- **Automate the checking, not just the doing:** one Workers cron sends you a daily one-line health digest (checkout webhook alive · Stream playable · last night's ingest status · yesterday's sales count); errors email you (the `reportError` seam already exists); a weekly D1 dump runs on schedule (the repo's own dump-before-anything discipline, automated). You should learn about breakage from a green/red line over coffee, never from a student's angry Zalo message.

## 9. Kill / reshape criteria (pre-committed, so emotions don't vote later)
**Why this section exists:** when sales disappoint, the untrained reaction is either "quit" or "push harder on everything." Both are wrong. Your sales machine has exactly three stages — **attention → conversation → payment** — and a bad month means ONE of them is leaking, not all of them. These rules are written NOW, while you're calm, so that on a bad day you read the gauges like a mechanic instead of gambling like a panicked founder. **Reshape = keep the venture, rebuild the one broken part. Kill = stop entirely.** They are very different moves.

**The three reshape gauges — find which stage leaks, change ONLY that:**

| What you observe | Which part is broken | What you change (and nothing else) |
|---|---|---|
| You asked 100 real prospects directly; fewer than 5 paid a deposit | **The OFFER.** People hear the promise and shrug — wrong promise, wrong audience, or wrong packaging. More asking won't fix it. | Rework the promise with the instructor: different transformation ("job offer in Japan" vs "pass N3"), different audience slice (final-year students vs working engineers), stronger guarantee, different format (cohort vs self-paced). | People DO book consultation calls — but at the end, they don't pay | **The CLOSE.** Interest is real; the last step scares them: price above their trust level, weak guarantee, payment friction, no deadline. | Fix closing mechanics only: founding price, installments (trả góp), stronger cam kết đầu ra, "500K₫ deposit holds your seat", enrollment deadline. | Views and followers grow steadily — but nobody books a consult | **The BRIDGE.** You've attracted the wrong people (entertainment viewers, not buyers), or there's no clear path from video → Zalo → call. | Fix funnel plumbing only: clearer call-to-action on every post, better lead magnet, shift content topics toward what working engineers (the buyers) search for. |

**The two kill switches (rare, absolute — everything above is repairable, these are not):**
- **Kill the cohort FORMAT** (fall back to self-paced course + membership) if the instructor can't sustain the live cadence for 2 consecutive cohorts. This kills a format, not the business.

## 9.5 Noise vs. Signal — when to keep pushing, when to stop (the emotional firewall)

*"You could be eleven noes away — but if you stop at the tenth rejection, you will have failed"* — and the mirror: eleven noes on the SAME objection is a pattern demanding a fix, not more persistence. **Persistence is for volume; adaptation is for patterns; gates are for verdicts.**
