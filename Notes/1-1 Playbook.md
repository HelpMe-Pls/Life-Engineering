# 1-1 Playbook: Renewal, Base, SHUI

> [!warning] Private file. Never open on a work screen. Numbers inside.

**What this meeting is:** NOT asking for a raise. Two sides deciding the **next contract**. He needs a story he can feel good about. You need numbers on paper. Trade his story for your numbers.

**The one rule:** every move here still works even if he knows the trick. If a move only works while hidden, it's not in this file. ([[Never Split The Difference]]: tactical empathy, not lies.)

---
## ⚡ THE 2-MINUTE CARD (read this right before walking in)

**Goal stack:**
1. Base ≥ **59** gross now (floor **47.5**, never spoken)
2. Social insurance **current + dated schedule** (VssID)
3. Written path to **65**

**5 rules:**
1. **Speak gross. Always.** (contract language)
2. ==**Silence is your weapon.** After every number and question: shut up. Count 4 seconds.== He fills silence. That's him negotiating with himself.
3. **Slow voice, smile.** Late-night radio DJ. Slow = "I'm in control."
4. **Never defend. Label instead.** "Sounds like that frustrated you." Then quiet.
5. **His "yeah, ok, fine" = nothing.** Only dates and amounts he repeats back = real. ==A "Yes" without a "how" is fake.==

**3 lines to know cold (say them exactly like this):**
- 🥇 **The band:** *"I've done my research and found that the market rate for my role right now is 58.5 to 64.5, realistically speaking"*
- 🥈 **The gate:** *"How am I supposed to sign another year at half the market while I'm capable of carrying three people's work?"*
- 🥉 **The walk:** *"I'm sorry. I'm afraid I just can't do that."* (then NOTHING. Not one more word.)

**3 traps:**
- A fast happy "yes" from you = money left on the table. Even at a great number: pause, then accept slowly.
- Arguing facts against his feelings. He goes emotional → you label first, facts later.
- Accepting a "review later" promise. Not written into the contract = doesn't exist.

## 3. The script: 8 beats

> Beats, not a screenplay. He WILL go off-road → grab the matching line from §4 and come back.
### Beat 1: Warm-up, feed the teacher (2 min, genuine)
**SAY:** "Morning [Name]! How it's going? I hope everything is going well with AIO."
**DO:** Let him bark. Real curiosity. 

### Beat 2: Set the table (30 sec)
**SAY:** "Thanks for making the time. So, my contract ends in October, and I'd like us to get aligned on the next one today. I've got two things on my list. The insurance paperwork, and the base for the new contract. Where do you want to start?"
**WHY:** He picks the order = he feels in control. You set the menu = you control what's on it.

### Beat 3: The honest question (max 3 min, your original opener, kept)
**SAY:** "Before we get into numbers, there's one honest question I've been sitting on. What's it actually been like for you, having me on the team this year?"
**DO:** LISTEN. Mirror once ("...communication?"). Label once ("Sounds like that part frustrated you."). Then: **"That's fair."** And STOP.
==Never defend. Not one rebuttal.== His complaints surfacing NOW at minute 5 (cheap) beats them surfacing at minute 25 as the reason you don't deserve 59.
**EXIT LINE:** "Thank you. That's honest, and I can work with all of it."

### Beat 4: The pivot, generalist callback → seat repricing (the one "guess" you keep)
**SAY:** "Do you remember the first question you ever asked me, back in our first meeting?"
*(if he remembers, great, he's co-authoring; if not:)* "You asked me if I wanted to be a specialist or a generalist. I picked generalist. That was your advice. Honestly, the best advice I got here."
**THEN:** "And since then, I've taken ownership of the EOVN setup, the rollout tracker, the Altai sync, and the whole EO HubSpot thing. So the seat I'm sitting in today is not the seat you hired me into. It grew. That's what today is really about. So what I'm proposing is less of a raise and more of pricing the seat as it is now."
**WHY:** His own doctrine (generalist) becomes the premise → the conclusion (market price for a generalist seat) inherits his authorship. He can't attack it without attacking himself.

### Beat 5: The good-intention prime (20 sec)
**SAY:** "You know my favorite line in the onboarding doc? Assume good intention. I've always seen you in that light. So I'm asking for the same read today. Everything I bring up in this room means I want to stay and build. Not leave."
**WHY:** Kills the "ungrateful mercenary" story before his brain writes it.

### Beat 6: SHUI, logistics not negotiation (5 min, dates only)
**SAY:** "Let's do the easy one first. And honestly, it's not really a negotiation. It's just the current contract. Health and unemployment are running again. I noticed, and thank you for pushing that through." *(credit = face)*
**THEN:** "Social insurance is the last piece. It's been nine months, and if the math is right, that's practically a whole month's worth of my pay. What does the schedule look like on your side, so it's current before we sign the new one?"
**DO:** Whatever he offers → pin it: **"So [amount] showing in VssID by [date], and the rest by [date]. I'll put both in my calendar."**
**IF he links it to the raise:** "This one is the old contract. The new base is a separate conversation. Let's not mix the two."
**Hard lines (unchanged June law):** amount fixed, only the schedule moves · fund remittance only, visible in VssID, never cash in hand · current-from-next-payroll is not a chip at ANY salary.

### Beat 7: The number (the main event)
**Transition:** "Okay, now the bigger one. You've seen what the seat carries these days. For the renewal, what base do you have in mind?"
**DO:** ==SILENCE. Let him say a number first. However long it takes.==

Then play the card:

| He opens                               | You do                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **≥ 59**                               | Pause 3 sec (no grin). "That tells me you see what I see. Let's lock it." → Beat 8. Do NOT push to 65. Bank the 65-path instead (below).                                                                     |
| **47.5–58.9**                          | "I appreciate that. That's real movement. Here's my problem though. 🥇 *I've done my research and the market for this seat right now is 58.5 to 64.5.* How do we get to the bottom of that band?" → SILENCE. |
| **< 47.5 / "no raise" / percent-talk** | 🥈 *"How am I supposed to sign another year at half the market rate while I'm capable of carrying three people's work?"* → SILENCE. Then the band.                                                           |
| **Refuses to name first**              | Quote the band as the market's number, not yours: "Openings for this seat are running 58.5 to 64.5. Where can Edge8 land in that band?"                                                                      |

**Ackerman ladder** (if bargaining down from the top; shrinking steps, never round the last one):
**64.5 → 61.3 → 59.9 → 59.35 final** *(pause 5 sec before 59.35, slow voice: "That's genuinely the limit of what I can sign.")*

**The 65-path (fire AFTER landing ≥59, or while grinding):**
**SAY:** "And just so I know the road ahead. What would you need to see from me to make 65 an easy yes?"
**DO:** He lists things → repeat his list back until he says "that's right" → **"Can we write that in as a review on [date]?"** *(His criteria = his commitment. Advice given = he now wants you to succeed.)*

**Escalator fallback. ONLY after the grind stalls below 59 twice:**
**SAY:** "Okay, here's a bridge. [55] now, and we write it into the contract. [61] on January 1st, if I hit the things you just listed. Not a promise, an actual contract line. Does that work on your end?"
==A soft "we'll review it later" = counterfeit. Written, dated, numbered, or it doesn't exist.==

**Below 52 after everything:** "I hear you. Let's both sleep on it. I don't want one sitting to decide a whole year. Thursday, same time?" *(Adjourn ≠ retreat. The calendar works for you until October.)*

**Below 47.5, final answer:** 🥉 *"I'm sorry. I'm afraid I just can't do that."* Then nothing. Hold the silence until HE speaks.

### Beat 8: Close the package
**SAY:** "Let me play that back to make sure I've got it right. Base [59] from [Sep 1]. Social insurance current by [date], on the schedule you set. And the 65 review written in for [date]. Did I get all of that right?"
**GOAL:** He restates or corrects = real agreement (that's a "that's right," not a "you're right").
**The bow:** "Honestly? This is the version of Edge8 you teach about. I'd love to be that story."
**Paper:** "I'll drop a two-line recap in chat today so it's all in one place. And can we get the renewal draft going this week?"
==Anything he hands you to sign: "Let me read it properly tonight." Never sign same-day.==

## 8. Voice & tone (the 7-38-55 layer)
- Numbers land in the **slow, low, downward** voice. Everything else: light and warm.
- **4 seconds of silence** after every label, mirror, question, and number. The silence does the work. Don't rescue him from it. He MUST be the one who breaks the silence, not you.
- One label per move. Never two questions in a row. Never answer your own question.
- If YOU get emotional: slow down, drop volume, smile. (You can't influence his emotions while yours are driving.)
- His mood decides the channel ([[Supercommunicators]]): he goes emotional → label feelings first, park the data. He goes practical → numbers and dates. Match, then lead.

### He's American: register notes
- **First name, always.** Casual-direct IS the culture. A clean, direct ask reads as competence; hedging and hinting read like you're hiding something. You will not offend him by naming a number. You'd offend him by dancing around it.
- **Don't over-flatter.** Americans smell flattery in seconds and it flips them to guard mode, the exact thing we're avoiding. The ego-feed runs on ==genuine curiosity== (ask about the retreat, let him teach, ask follow-ups), never on compliments.
- **Two doctrine-callbacks max** (generalist + assume-good-intention). Three or more of his own phrases back at him and an American hears parody, not homage.
- **"Fair" is his word to say, not yours.** If he invokes fairness: "I want exactly that. That's why we're pricing the seat, not the history."
- **Humor lands.** Your natural playfulness is the disarm channel. One well timed joke beats three clever setups. Cut anything that feels like a riddle or a quiz; Americans hate being Socratic'd by someone they outrank. (This is why the script keeps only ONE callback question, Beat 4, and it's one he can't get wrong.)
- **The happy-exit design:** he must leave feeling he made a *smart business call*, not that he lost one. The receipts folder hands him tomorrow's story: "market repriced the role, I kept my best generalist. Retention, like I teach it." Give him that sentence and he'll repeat it as his own.

### Speak it, don't recite it
- Every SAY line here is a skeleton, not scripture. Say it in your own rhythm. If a word feels stiff in your mouth during rehearsal, swap it and keep the *move*.
- Short sentences survive pressure. Long ones collapse. When in doubt, cut the sentence in half and let the pause do the connecting.
- Fillers are human: "look", "honestly", "so, here's the thing". One per line max. They buy you thinking time and sound native.
