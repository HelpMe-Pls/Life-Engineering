# Freestyle swimming speed and endurance standards for a 28-year-old man (57 kg, 170 cm)

Key: `swim-endurance`. Domain: freestyle speed/endurance standards for an adult man aged 28. Body mass is not a normalizer in swimming; the 57 kg / small-frame facts appear only in Caveats. Tier labels used exactly as defined by the brief: **Above average** (better than ~60–75% of trained adult men), **Strong & athletic** (better than ~85–95%), **Elite** (top 1–5%; national-class amateur / USMS NQT level). Where a source uses its own labels I state the mapping.

Unit conventions: 1 yd = 0.9144 m (exact), so 100 yd = 91.44 m, 300 yd = 274.3 m, 500 yd = 457.2 m, 550 yd = 502.9 m, 1000 yd = 914.4 m, 1650 yd = 1508.8 m. Pace is written min:sec per 100 m unless "/100 yd" is stated. "SCY" = 25-yard pool, "SCM" = 25-m pool, "LCM" = 50-m pool.

## Findings

### A. Cooper 12-minute swim test and its validation

1. **Cooper (1982) rating table, men, distance in 12 minutes (any stroke), as reproduced by #4 (identical figures on #5).** Population basis: Cooper's aerobics program tables; the reproductions do not state N. Men 20–29: Excellent >700 yd (>640 m); Good 600–699 yd (549–639 m); Fair 500–599 yd (457–548 m); Poor 400–499 yd (366–456 m); Very poor <400 yd (<366 m). Men 30–39: Excellent >650 yd (>594 m); Good 550–649 yd (503–593 m); Fair 450–549 yd (411–502 m); Poor 350–449 yd (320–410 m); Very poor <350 yd (<320 m). Arithmetic: yd × 0.9144; e.g., 700 × 0.9144 = 640.1 m; 600 × 0.9144 = 548.6 m. Pace equivalents for a 28-year-old (20–29 band): "Excellent" threshold 640 m/12 min = 1:52.5 per 100 m; "Good" floor 549 m = 2:11 per 100 m; "Fair" floor 457 m = 2:37.6 per 100 m. The primary (#3) could not be opened, so these figures are **verified only as a reproduction** (two independent reproductions agree). [#3 UNVERIFIED primary; #4, #5]

2. **Validation in men (Conley, Cureton, Dengel & Weyand 1991, #1).** 36 young men (male recreational swimmers, 1991, USA). Mean 12-min swim 581 ± 88 m (= 635 yd, i.e., Cooper "Good"); 12-min run 2797 ± 290 m; tethered-swim VO2peak 50.3 ± 6.2 and treadmill VO2peak 57.2 ± 5.5 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹. Correlation of 12-min swim distance with tethered-swim VO2peak r = 0.40 (SEE 5.7 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) and with treadmill VO2peak r = 0.38 (SEE 5.1); the 12-min run predicted far better (r = 0.74 and 0.88). Conclusion quoted: the 12-min swim "has relatively low validity as a field test of peak aerobic power … may be adequate for fitness classification in situations in which a high level of accuracy is not needed." No regression equation is given in the abstract; the full text was not accessible. [#1]

3. **Validation in women (Conley et al. 1992, #2).** 34 young women (female recreational swimmers). 12-min swim 597 ± 82 m; 12-min run 2313 ± 317 m; tethered-swim VO2peak 39.2 ± 4.9; treadmill VO2peak 45.4 ± 6.3 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹. r = 0.42 (SEE 4.5) for tethered-swim VO2peak from the 12-min swim; r = 0.34 (SEE 6.0) for treadmill VO2peak. Same conclusion: low validity relative to the 12-min run. [#2]

4. **Interpretation for the tiers.** Conley's college men (a young, active but non-swimmer population) averaged Cooper "Good"; +1 SD (669 m) reaches "Excellent". The 1982 categories are therefore general-population/recreational norms: Cooper "Good" (549–639 m) ≈ the brief's **Above average**, "Excellent" (>640 m) is only the *floor* of **Strong & athletic** for a trained swimmer. Elite masters swimmers cover far more: the USMS 1000-yd NQT pace (Finding 20) equals 1,045 m in 12 minutes. [#1, #4, #6]

### C. Lifeguard and lifesaving prerequisites

### D. Triathlon age-group swim data (men 25–29)

20. **Peer-reviewed IRONMAN data.** #20 (2025; 687,696 IRONMAN age-group race records 2002–2022 per its methods, 553,608 men): men 25–29 n = 42,310, mean overall race time 12:19:28 ± 1:47:18; fastest male age group 30–34 (12:15:21). [#18, #20, #21]

### G. Test definition and course conversions

29. **Recommended test protocol (my specification, using the rulebook only as reference).** 25 m pool (or 50 m; state which), water push start from the wall (no dive; a dive start is worth roughly 1–2 s over 100 m — convention, not sourced), freestyle with legal turns (wall touch with any body part; surfacing within 15 m of each wall per World Aquatics SW 5 — rule not opened this session, #59), continuous, timed with the first wall touch at the finish. ## Caveats

- **Body size and buoyancy (subject 57 kg, BMI 19.7, 170 cm, small frame).** Swimming standards are not mass-normalized; none of the sources scales by body weight. Low body fat lowers buoyancy (legs ride lower, more drag), and shorter stature/arm span reduces distance per stroke; both argue that sprint times will be relatively harder for this subject than endurance pace, and that technique (body position, kick timing) matters more than in running. No opened source quantifies this for adults of his size, so it stays qualitative.
- **Validity of the 12-minute swim.** In young men it correlates only r ≈ 0.40 with swimming VO2peak (SEE ≈ 5.7 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) and r ≈ 0.38 with treadmill VO2peak (Conley 1991); distance is dominated by technique. Use it as a swim-fitness benchmark, not as a VO2max estimate; Galpin's "12-minute test" is the running version.
- **Cooper norms are 1982 general-population tables reproduced second-hand** (the book itself could not be opened) and allow any stroke; the freestyle-only convention used here makes the Cooper thresholds slightly easier than for a breaststroke-only swimmer.
- **Course and start.** All pool figures assume a 25 m pool and a push start; 25-yd times are 8.56% shorter in distance; 50 m pools are ≈ 2–4% slower by convention (turn count), and the conversion factors quoted (×1.11, +1–3%) were not verified against a rulebook this session.
- **Open-water tiers are secondary.** The 1.5 km bands rest on coach/industry benchmarks (220 Triathlon, Polar) and one elite peer-reviewed cut-off; wetsuits, drafting, currents and sighting make open-water times non-comparable with pool times.
- **USMS 2026 NQT age group is unlabelled** on the PDF; the explanatory page (usms.org) was unreachable. Treat "national-class ≈ NQT" as Elite for a man in his late 20s but verify the age-group applicability.
- **Military numbers.** Only the Royal Navy, ADF, USMC MARADMIN and QPS pages were opened; all U.S. Navy, Air Force, Army and Coast Guard numbers are reproduced from a secondary page (#5) or from search snippets and are flagged UNVERIFIED. Navy/AF swims are in side/breast stroke and are minimum entry screens, not athletic standards; "competitive" applicant times are informal.
- **Population mismatch.** Mandsager's percentiles come from clinic referrals (mean age 53, 45% smokers), so "elite" (≥ 55 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ for men 20–29) is roughly the *mean* of Conley's healthy college men (57.2); trained young men should expect to sit in the top two Mandsager bands. Masters records and NQTs describe competing swimmers, a self-selected population much faster than lap swimmers.
- **Age.** Peak endurance-swimming performance in masters data is at 35–40 (Tanaka & Seals) and the IRONMAN swim decline starts at 25–29 (Käch) — no age discount applies to a 28-year-old.
