## Findings

1. **USMC PFT pull-up scoring, male 26-30: UNVERIFIED.** Both the official page (#1) and its archive copy (#2) were unreachable. From memory of the MCO 6100.13A scoring tables introduced in 2017 (and, as far as I recall, unchanged for pull-ups in CH-3, 2022): male 26-30 minimum = 5 pull-ups, 100 points = 23 pull-ups; form rule = dead hang with arms fully extended, chin above the bar, no kipping or leg swing. The verifier must confirm against https://www.fitness.marines.mil/PFT-CFT_Standards17/ or the MCO PDF; treat the "23 for max" as the single most important cross-check number in this file because it is the only population-wide, judged, maximal-score reference for strict pull-ups.
2. **Navy SEAL/SWCC PST pull-ups: UNVERIFIED.** #3 redirected and #4 returned 403. From memory of the sealswcc.com table: SEAL minimum 10 pull-ups (no time limit, dead hang, no kip), "competitive" 15-20; SWCC minimum 6. Also from memory: push-ups min 50 / competitive 80-100 in 2 min; curl-ups min 50 / competitive 80-100 in 2 min; 500 yd swim min 12:30 / competitive 9:00-9:30. Verifier to confirm.
3. **Air Force Special Warfare pull-ups: UNVERIFIED.** #5 and #6 unreachable. From memory of the Initial Fitness Test (IFT) for PJ/CCT/SR/TACP: pull-ups minimum 8 in 1 minute (dead hang, no kip); plus 2 x 25 m underwaters, 500 m swim, 1.5 mi run, sit-ups and push-ups in 2 min. Verifier to confirm the exact per-AFSC numbers; the pull-up minimum is the only figure used here.
4. **Pull-up performance vs body mass (search snippets W1, W2; PubMed #7 not opened).** Vanderburgh's line of work (Vanderburgh & Flanagan 2000, Military Medicine, "The backpack run test"; Vanderburgh 2008, MSSE, "Occupational relevance and body mass bias in military physical fitness tests") shows that unloaded calisthenic tests such as pull-ups systematically penalise heavier service members, with the size of the penalty explained by allometric scaling (muscle force scales roughly with mass^(2/3) while the load lifted in a pull-up is mass^1). Implication for a 57 kg man: the same relative strength that gives an 85 kg man ~12 pull-ups gives him ~15-16 (ratio (85/57)^(1/3) ~ 1.14, applied to reps is only a rough proxy). Practical consequence: use bodyweight-specific tables (Strength Level's 55/60 kg rows, S1) and expect pull-up counts at the upper end of any population-wide military table. Exact effect sizes are NOT verified here because the abstract did not load; the "2/3" exponent is the standard geometric-similarity assumption, not a quoted value from the paper.
5. **CrossFit Open movement standards (search snippets W3, W4; PDF #10 timed out).** Air squat: hip crease clearly below the top of the knee at the bottom, full hip and knee extension at the top. Bar muscle-up: must begin with or pass through a hang below the bar with arms fully extended and feet off the ground; kipping allowed in the Open, but pull-overs, rolls to support and glide kips are not; rep credited when arms are fully locked out in support above the bar with shoulders over or slightly in front of the bar. Ring muscle-up: start hanging with arms fully extended and feet off the ground; consecutive kipping reps need a change of direction below the rings; the rep must pass through some portion of a dip before lockout; credited when elbows are fully locked out in the support position. For the subject's "immaculate form" goal, add "no kip, no leg swing, from a motionless dead hang" to each.
6. **Overcoming Gravity / r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine: UNVERIFIED (#9 blocked).** From memory: the RR progresses pull-ups and dips through 3 x 5-8 per step and moves to the next step at 3 x 8; common muscle-up prerequisites quoted in that community are ~8-10 strict pull-ups (ideally chest-to-bar) plus ~8-10 strict dips before training the transition, with the strict muscle-up placed at roughly level 7-8 of Steven Low's 16-level charts. Not used as a numeric basis in the table; used only to justify treating "strict" muscle-ups as one tier harder than Strength Level's un-qualified "muscle-ups".
7. **Origin of the squat-test norms (#8, opened OK).** Topend's "Squat Test" is a sit-to-chair squat to fatigue (chair set so the knees are at ~90 degrees, hands on hips, light touch of the chair), NOT a 1-minute test and NOT a full-depth squat. Male 20-29 ratings verbatim: Excellent >34, Good 33-34, Above Average 30-32, Average 27-29, Below Average 24-26, Poor 21-23, Very Poor <21; male 30-39: Excellent >32, Good 30-32, Above Average 27-29, Average 24-26, Below Average 21-23, Poor 18-20, Very Poor <18. Topend states: "Table source: originally taken from Total Fitness ebook, by Vince Antonetti. Data corrected and expanded", citing Antonetti, V. (1999), "Total Fitness: A Home-Based Program for Measuring and Improving Your Fitness Level", self-published. So the trail ends at a self-published home-fitness ebook with no stated sample; rate LOW CONFIDENCE and treat as general-population, not trained. The brianmac page was not opened (budget); it is widely reported to reproduce similar numbers, which if true means both sites share this single untraceable origin.
8. **Peer-reviewed bodyweight-squat repetitions-to-failure norms in adults: none found.** The search (query logged in the run) surfaced only reliability work on loaded squats (ICC ~0.65-0.86 for repetitions at 70-90% 1RM) and a single-leg timed-squat reliability figure (ICC 0.80 for a 50-rep timed test, 0.89 for a 20-second test), with no adult normative table for unloaded squats to failure. Treat the Strength Level bodyweight-squat page (S1) as the only large-sample reference, with the caveat that its entries are unjudged.
9. **Convict Conditioning progression standards (W7 snippets; #12 failed).** Snippet-confirmed: Full Pull-ups (Step 5) progression standard 2 x 10; Full Squats progression standard 3 x 30; Step 10 of each series is the one-arm pull-up and the one-leg squat. From memory, UNVERIFIED: Full Pull-ups beginner 1 x 5, intermediate 2 x 8; One-arm Pull-up 1 x 1 / 2 x 3 / 2 x 6; Full Squats 1 x 5 / 2 x 10 / 3 x 30; One-leg Squat 1 x 5 / 2 x 10 / 2 x 50. These are training-progression gates, not population percentiles, so they anchor only the "Elite" end (2 x 50 pistols per leg, 2 x 6 one-arm pull-ups).
10. **Why rep-max formulas fail beyond ~10-15 reps (W5, W6 snippets).** Reynolds et al. 2006 (JSCR 20(3):584-592; n = 70, bench press and leg press, 5RM/10RM/20RM) found 5RM gave the best prediction (R^2 = 0.993 bench, 0.974 leg press) with accuracy degrading substantially at 20RM, and concluded that no more than 10 repetitions should be used in linear 1RM-prediction equations. Mayhew et al. 2008 (n = 103 women) likewise found equations more accurate when repetitions to failure were <=10. Mechanism: above ~10 reps the set is limited by local muscular endurance and metabolic factors rather than maximal force, and the load-repetition relationship flattens, so a 20-rep set tells you little about 1RM. Consequence for this file: Strength Level's weighted-pull-up and bodyweight-pull-up tables are not interconvertible; 24 strict pull-ups does not imply a particular weighted 1RM.

## Caveats

- Strength Level rows (S1) are self-reported, unjudged, and skewed towards people who train calisthenics; "Intermediate" there means "stronger than 50% of users", which is below the brief's 60-75% definition of Above average, so the Above-average column may be slightly generous.
- Every military figure (Findings 1-3) is from memory and flagged UNVERIFIED; none of the six official/archive URLs opened. The USMC 100-point figure of 23 for 26-30 is the one that most needs confirmation because it is used as the cross-check for the Strong & athletic pull-up cell.
- The muscle-up rows are judgment mappings, not measured percentiles. Strength Level does not separate strict from kipping or bar from rings, and no peer-reviewed or community dataset for strict muscle-up counts at a given bodyweight was found.
- The 60-second squat row has no source at all; it is derived from pacing and should be treated as a placeholder until the verifier finds a published timed norm.
- The topend squat norms trace to a self-published 1999 ebook with no sample described (Finding 7) and test a 90-degree chair-touch squat; they must not be mixed into the full-depth rows.
- Pull-up and dip tiers scale with body mass (Finding 4): the subject's 57 kg lowers the absolute bar on loaded lifts but raises it on unloaded reps; the Strength Level bodyweight rows already embed this, so no further correction was applied.
- Rep-max conversion between weighted and bodyweight pull-ups is invalid beyond ~10 reps (Finding 10); the weighted-pull-up row was omitted rather than estimated.
- Search-snippet sources (W1-W7) were never opened; quotations attributed to them are the search engine's summary and may be paraphrased.
