# Butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke and individual medley: standards for a 28-year-old man

Key: `strokes`. Domain: the three non-freestyle strokes and the individual medley (IM) for an adult man aged 28, 170 cm, 57 kg. Freestyle is covered in [[SWIMMING]]; its Standards table (100 free: Above average 1:20–1:30, Strong & athletic 1:08–1:15, Elite ≤ 1:00 with national-class ≈ 0:54–0:56; 200 free: 3:00–3:20 / 2:30–2:45 / ≤ 2:12; all 25 m pool, push start) is the anchor that this note scales by stroke. Body mass is not a normalizer anywhere in swimming; the 57 kg fact appears only in Caveats. Tier labels are used exactly as the brief defines them: **Above average** = better than ~60–75% of trained adult men; **Strong & athletic** = better than ~85–95%; **Elite** = top ~1–5% (national-class amateur; USMS National Qualifying Time level). Each tier states how the source's own labels were mapped.

## Findings

### A. What counts as a legal stroke (World Aquatics, Part Two Swimming Rules, 2025) [#1]

1. **Backstroke (Art. 6).** Start in the water facing the starting end, both hands on the grips; after the start and each turn the swimmer "shall push off and swim upon their back throughout the race" except during the turn; the normal position on the back may include a body roll "up to, but not including 90 degrees from horizontal" (head position irrelevant). Some part of the swimmer must break the surface throughout, except that the swimmer may be fully submerged during the turn and "for a distance of not more than 15 metres after the start and each turn", and once the head has passed the 5 m mark before the finish. At the turn, some part of the body must touch the wall; the shoulders may turn past vertical to the breast followed by "an immediate continuous single arm pull or immediate continuous simultaneous double arm pull" to initiate the turn; the swimmer must leave the wall on the back. Finish: touch while on the back. [#1, Art. 6.1–6.5]

2. **Breaststroke (Art. 7).** After the start and each turn one arm stroke "completely back to the legs" is allowed (may be submerged) and "a single butterfly kick is permitted" at any time before the first breaststroke kick; the head must break the surface before the hands turn inward at the widest part of the second stroke. Body on the breast from the first arm stroke; rolling onto the back is forbidden except at the turn after the wall touch. "The stroke cycle must be one arm stroke and one leg kick in that order"; all arm and leg movements simultaneous, no alternating movement. Hands pushed forward together from the breast on, under or over the water; elbows under water except for the last stroke before the turn, during the turn and at the finish; hands not brought back beyond the hip line except in the first stroke after the start and each turn. Head must break the surface during each complete cycle. Feet "must be turned outwards during the propulsive part of the kick"; no alternating movements or downward butterfly kicks (except Art. 7.1). Turn and finish: touch "with both hands separated and simultaneously at, above, or below the water level"; "separated" means not stacked, no visible gap required. [#1, Art. 7.1–7.6]

3. **Butterfly (Art. 8).** Body on the breast from the first arm stroke after the start and each turn; no rolling onto the back except at the turn after the touch. "Both arms shall be brought forward simultaneously over the water and brought backward simultaneously under the water through-out the race." All up-and-down leg movements simultaneous; legs or feet need not be at the same level but may not alternate; breaststroke kick is not permitted. Turn and finish: both hands separated and simultaneous, at, above or below the surface. At the start and turns "one or more leg kicks and one arm pull under the water" are permitted; fully submerged for "not more than 15 metres after the start and after each turn", after which the head must have broken the surface and "the swimmer must remain on the surface until the next turn or finish." [#1, Art. 8.1–8.5]

4. **Individual medley (Art. 9).** Order "Butterfly, Backstroke, Breaststroke and Freestyle. Each of the strokes must cover one quarter (1/4) of the distance." In the freestyle leg the swimmer must be on the breast except when turning; leaving the wall on the back after the breaststroke-to-freestyle turn is allowed but no kick until past vertical to the breast. "Each section must be finished in accordance with the rule which applies to the stroke concerned." (Medley relay order is back-breast-fly-free, Art. 9.3.) Art. 10.5 applies to every event: the turn must be made from the wall with physical contact; no stride from the bottom. [#1, Art. 9.1–9.4, 10.5]

5. **Test-protocol implication (my specification).** "Immaculate form" for this note means: every length satisfies Art. 6/7/8 as paraphrased above (two-hand touches in fly and breast, no alternating kicks, on the back for backstroke, ≤ 15 m underwater per wall), the IM is swum in fly-back-breast-free order with each stroke finished under its own rule, and the swim starts with a push from the wall rather than a dive. Rule-legal technique is the entry condition for every tier below, not a tier in itself.

### E. Learn-to-swim frameworks: the floor for "can swim all four strokes" [#10, #11]

18. **Swim England Learn to Swim Stage 7 (official page, opened).** Stage 7 is the first and only core stage that requires all four strokes: "Push and glide and swim 25 metres backstroke / front crawl / breaststroke / butterfly (performed to Swim England expected standards)"; "Push and glide and swim 50 metres continuously using one stroke"; "Push and glide and swim 100 metres, using a minimum of three different strokes"; plus a one-minute movement sequence in a group, a sitting dive or dive, 30 s eggbeater tread, and an obstacle course with feet off the floor. No times are set; the criterion is technique "to Swim England expected standards". An adult who cannot pass Stage 7 is below every tier in this note. [#10]

19. **American Red Cross Learn-to-Swim (reproduction only, #11).** The opened team page lists "Level VI: Skill Proficiency — Front & back crawl 100 yards; Breaststroke & sidestroke 25 yards; Swim the butterfly 10 yards; Flip turn" and "Level VI: Advanced Skills — Swim continuously 500 yards using any strokes; Backstroke flip turn; Tread water for 5 minutes" (an older edition of the ARC program). Search snippets for the current ARC Level 6 (front crawl 100 yd, elementary backstroke 100 yd, back crawl 50 yd, breaststroke 50 yd, butterfly 50 yd, with open and flip turns for each stroke) were not confirmed on an opened page — UNVERIFIED. ### F. Course conversion and peak age [#12, #13]

20. **SCY / SCM / LCM conversion (convention; no governing-body source opened).** MySwimSplits states "SCM time = SCY time × 1.11", "LCM time = SCY time × 1.11" and "LCM time = SCM time + (SCM time × 0.03)", "based on methodologies recognized by the NCAA and USA Swimming" [#13]. The exact distance ratio is 100 m ÷ 100 yd = 1.0936; the extra ~1.5% in 1.11 compensates for the pace cost of the longer distance. Search summaries (not opened pages) add that USA Swimming and Swimming Canada do not formally endorse conversion factors and publish separate standards per course, and that LCM runs ~1.5% slower than SCM over 50 m, ~2% over 100 m and ~4% over 1500 m. Internal check from #5: boys-18 B cuts, 100 fly SCY 1:05.59 × 1.11 = 1:12.8 vs the published SCM 1:12.49 (0.4% off); 100 breast 1:14.89 × 1.11 = 1:23.1 vs 1:22.79; the SCM→LCM gap in the same rows is 3.5% (100 fly) to 5.2% (100 breast), wider than the 3% convention for breaststroke. In this note every table figure is for a 25 m pool; add ≈ 3–5% for a 50 m pool. [#5, #13; SwimTopia UNVERIFIED]

21. **Age of peak performance (Allen, Vandenbogaerde & Hopkins 2014, abstract via Europe PMC).** 683 swimmers who were top-16 in a pool event at the 2008 or 2012 Olympics, 6,959 annual best times, 13 events per sex, individual quadratic trajectories. "Men achieved peak performance later than women (24.2 ± 2.1 vs. 22.5 ± 2.4 years)" and "peak performance occurred at later ages for the shorter distances for both sexes (∼1.5–2.0 years between sprint and distance-event groups)"; the window of peak performance was 2.6 ± 1.5 years for both sexes. At 28 the subject is ~4 years past the elite male peak, well inside the 25–29 masters band, and masters data for freestyle ([[SWIMMING]] Findings 26–27, Tanaka & Seals; Rubin & Rahe) show decline of only ~0.6–0.8%/yr before 70. No age discount applies. [#12]

### G. Synthesis: how the tiers were set

22. **Method.** Freestyle tiers are taken as given from [[SWIMMING]] (100 free 1:20–1:30 / 1:08–1:15 / ≤ 1:00; 200 free 3:00–3:20 / 2:30–2:45 / ≤ 2:12; 50 free is derived here as 36–40 / 31–34 / ≤ 28 from the 100 free tiers and the boys-18 SCM 50 free B 30.49 and BB 28.29). Each stroke tier = freestyle tier × a stroke factor chosen from the observed range: fly × 1.10–1.12, back × 1.12–1.14, breast × 1.25–1.28, 100 IM × 1.13–1.15, 200 IM × 1.13–1.15. The factors sit at the USMS-NQT (amateur) end of the range rather than the world-record end because Findings 9, 14 and 15 show the penalty widening as swimmers get less elite, and because amateur butterfly in particular collapses over 100 m. Elite floors are then cross-checked against USA Swimming boys-18 SCM BB (top 35% of competitive 18-year-olds), which the freestyle note already used as the Elite floor (100 free Elite ≤ 1:00 vs BB 1:02.29); national-class = USMS NQT × 1.11; ceiling = masters 25–29 SCM record.

23. **Mapping of source labels to the brief's tiers.** USA Swimming B/BB (top 55%/35% of registered competitive youth) ≈ **Elite** floor for an untrained-population comparison: a registered competitive swimmer is already in a small minority of adult men who swim, so the lower half of that group is comfortably inside the top 1–5% of trained adult men — the same mapping the freestyle note implies. USA Swimming A–AAAA and USMS NQT = national-class, the upper part of Elite. Masters world records = above Elite (ceiling). Swim England Stage 7 and ARC Level 6 = below Above average (competence floor). No source provides percentiles of trained adult men for stroke times; the Above average and Strong & athletic cells are therefore derived (freestyle anchor × stroke factor) and are flagged as such.

## Standards at 57 kg / age 28

## Caveats

- **Above average and Strong & athletic cells are derived, not measured.** No opened source publishes percentile distributions of stroke times for trained adult men. Those two columns are the freestyle tiers from [[SWIMMING]] (themselves coach/industry anchored) multiplied by stroke factors taken from record ratios; the verification pass should treat them as estimates with roughly ± 5% uncertainty, more for butterfly, where amateur technique varies enormously.
- **Population mismatch of the Elite anchors.** USA Swimming cuts describe USA Swimming-registered boys aged 18 (competitive youth), and the B/BB percentiles come from a secondary explainer (#7), not from USA Swimming's own page. USMS NQTs describe competing masters swimmers; whether the 2026 sheet is 25–29-specific is UNVERIFIED. Masters world records are best-ever worldwide. None is a sample of "trained adult men".
- **Printed oddities in the USA Swimming PDF.** Boys-18 100 back and 100 fly cuts are identical in SCY and SCM, and 50 back = 50 fly in SCY; this is reproduced as printed. The "*" flags on most cells are unexplained in the PDF.
- **Conversion factors are convention.** × 1.11 (SCY→SCM/LCM) and + 3% (SCM→LCM) come from a calculator site (#13); governing bodies publish separate standards per course. The internal check (Finding 20) shows breaststroke's SCM→LCM gap is ~5%, so breaststroke LCM targets should be loosened more than the generic 3%.
- **Open world records were taken from Wikipedia (#2)**; the World Aquatics records page was not opened. Records change frequently (the list shows marks from March and September 2026).
- **Body size and buoyancy (57 kg, BMI 19.7, 170 cm, small frame).** No source scales swim times by body mass. Low body fat and short stature cost most in sprint and butterfly (less buoyancy, less reach per stroke) and least in breaststroke, which rewards timing and leg power; this is qualitative.
- **Age.** Elite men peak at 24.2 ± 2.1 years (#12); at 28 the subject is just past the elite peak and inside the 25–29 masters band, with masters decline of < 1%/yr before 70 — no age adjustment was applied.
- **Form.** The tiers assume rule-legal strokes (Findings 1–4). Times swum with a one-hand touch, alternating fly kick, breaststroke pull-outs beyond the rule, or more than 15 m underwater are not comparable.
