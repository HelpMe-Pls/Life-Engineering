## Findings

Each finding: source #, position (Perform transcript pages carry **no chapter timestamps**, so position is given as transcript paragraph index in the extracted text; Huberman Lab pages give chapter marks), speaker, exact quote or tight paraphrase (marked ≈), data-vs-opinion, mapping to the five tests, value at 57 kg.

### A. Galpin, Perform Ep. 2 "Why Muscle Matters & How to Build It" (S11, 2024-06-19)

1. **Bench press = 1× bodyweight (men).** Paragraph 311 (immediately after the powerlifting world-record segment, roughly 90% through). Galpin: "For men, I'd like to see about a one to one ratio for the bench press, such that you should be able to bench press your body weight. So a 200-pound person should be aiming for something like 200-pound bench press. For women, it's about 0.6." Opinion ("rough guidelines", para 318). Maps to incline bench only indirectly (flat bench). **57 kg → 57 kg flat bench.** Note Strength Level 57 kg flat bench Intermediate is 68 kg, so Galpin's 1×BW sits between Novice (47–53) and Intermediate: it is a *floor*, not "Above average".

2. **Back squat: no number; leg press 2× bodyweight instead.** Paragraph 312. Galpin: "Now, for the back squat, it's hard to give you numbers because it is so technically demanding. I think it's easier to give you something more like a leg press value. For men, you want to shoot for something like double body weight. And for women, something like one and a half times or so." Opinion. Maps to barbell back squat (proxy only). **57 kg → 114 kg leg press.** Not convertible to a back-squat load.

3. **Grip strength >45 kg (men), asymmetry <10%.** Paragraphs 313–315. Galpin: "For men, just again a rough number here. I like to see individuals over 45 kilograms. And then for women, over 28 kilograms or so." and "asymmetry of more than 10%, specifically, increases your risks of sarcopenia and heightened denervation of muscle groups by 2.67 fold" (cites "recent papers", not named in transcript). Opinion for the cutoff; the 2.67-fold figure is data (uncited in the transcript; show notes list grip-strength/dementia cohort papers). **Revision vs 2023:** in the 2023 Huberman series (EXPERT-BENCHMARKS S1) Galpin said "minimum score... of 40 kilograms. Ideally, you're up past 60"; here the "like to see" floor is 45 kg. Maps to muscle-up prerequisite (grip), not body-weight-scaled.

4. **"Don't stop at bodyweight."** Paragraph 318. Galpin: "So those strength standards I gave you are rough guidelines. But I want you to get as strong as possible... But I don't want you to just stop at bodyweight either... there's no disadvantage to getting stronger." Opinion. Confirms the 1×BW bench is a floor.

5. **FFMI percentiles (men).** Paragraphs 206–208. Galpin: "For men to be in the 25th percentile. Your FFMI score would be about 17.9... 50th percentile... 19.1 or so... 75th, you've now gone up to 20.4 for men... I actually prefer people to be in the 95th percentile or higher... something like north of 22 for men." Para 188: "If you're at 23.5 for most men, this is going to put you in the 99th percentile"; para 187–188: 25 = "established limit, what we think to be natural", Arnold ≈28. Data (percentile tables; show notes link "Fat-free mass index (FFMI) percentiles by sex"). Not one of the five tests but it sets the ceiling on all of them: **at 170 cm, FFM = FFMI × 2.89 m²** → 25th pct 51.7 kg, 50th 55.2 kg, 75th 59.0 kg, 95th 63.6 kg fat-free mass. The subject weighs 57 kg total; at ~12% body fat his FFM is ≈50 kg → FFMI ≈17.4, i.e., **below Galpin's 25th percentile**. Reaching his "above average" (75th) needs ≈59 kg lean mass, i.e., ≈67 kg bodyweight at 12% fat.

6. **Muscle-mass asymmetry.** Paragraph 198 (≈): pay attention to side-to-side muscle-mass differences of more than about 10%. Opinion/data. General.

7. **Powerlifting world records as the "gold standard" (context only).** Paragraphs 298–310: Dave Hoff total 3,103 lb; Nathan Baptist squat 1,311 lb; Jimmy Kolb bench 1,350 lb; Andy Bolton deadlift 1,008 lb (all equipped). Data. Not applicable to a 57 kg raw lifter; recorded because Galpin frames his "realistic numbers" against them.

### B. Galpin, Perform Ep. 1 "How & Why to Strengthen Your Heart & Cardiovascular Fitness" (S9, 2024-06-12)

8. **VO2 max tiers (men).** Paragraphs 307–319. Galpin: "the average person... somewhat moderately trained, male or female, in the neighborhood of 170 pounds or 70 kilos... is probably going to have a VO2 max around 35 to 45 milliliters per kilogram per minute." "If you fall below 18... you're probably crossing below the threshold of what we call independence." Male 50–59 example: "above average would be 36 to 40... you'd need to be above 50 to be considered elite at that age." Personal rule: "there's no excuse to be under 50 unless you're over 50... And even then 50 to 60, I don't want you anywhere near below 50." Para 319: "those are technically elite and would probably put you in the 99th percentile... far higher than that 55 and 60 I just described." Para 101: MMA athletes average "about 55 milliliters per kilogram per minute." Data for the age-band numbers (he says to look up the charts; show notes link "Interpreting VO2 max numbers"); opinion for the "under 50" rule. **He does not read out the 20–29 male row.** Maps to swimming only as the aerobic-engine proxy (he gives no swim times). Value for a 28-year-old: floor 50 (his rule), "elite" per his own words ≈55–60. Not bodyweight-scaled (already ml/kg/min).

9. **Attia's "elite for a decade (or two) younger" rule, as relayed by Galpin.** Paragraphs 313–314. Galpin: "my friend Peter Attia likes to tell people he wants them to be considered elite in at least the decade before their actual age, if not two decades before." Opinion (second-hand). For a 28-year-old this collapses to "elite for 20–29", i.e., ≥55–60 by finding 8.

10. **Resting heart rate context.** Paragraph 294: elite endurance athletes "in the low 30s"; lowest Induráin 28 bpm. Data. General.

11. **Lab test preferred.** Paragraph 277: "spend a couple hundred dollars and get this actually tested in a laboratory... I have not yet seen really any standard over-the-counter fitness technology that gets an accurate" estimate. Opinion. Method note for the swimming/endurance row.

### D. Galpin, Perform Ep. 30 "Fundamentals of Training for Muscle, Strength & Longevity" (S12, 2026-07-22)

14. **Grip strength: train it if below ~30th percentile; chasing 80th→90th buys nothing.** Paragraphs 44–46. ## Caveats

1. **Every Galpin number here is absolute or BW-ratio for "the average person", not age- or frame-adjusted.** The grip (45 kg) and VO2 (50+) cutoffs are harder for a 57 kg, 14 cm-wrist man than for the 70–90 kg men they are written for; Galpin himself warned in 2023 that hang/grip tests are easier for light people, which cuts the other way for grip-in-kg.
2. **The FFMI finding dominates:** by Galpin's own percentiles the subject is under-muscled (≈17.4 vs 17.9 at the 25th percentile). Any "strong & athletic" strength tier at 57 kg bodyweight is, in his framework, being chased from below the muscle-mass floor; the honest first target is ≈59 kg lean mass.
3. **Perform transcripts carry no chapter timestamps.** Positions are paragraph indices in the extracted transcript text, not audio times; the verifier should search the quoted strings on the `/transcript/` pages.
4. **VO2 tiers for age 28 are extrapolated** from Galpin's 50–59 male example and his "no excuse under 50" rule; he explicitly defers the exact age-band table to charts in the show notes ("Interpreting VO2 max numbers" link on S9), which were not opened.
5. **None of the twelve sources gives a swimming, muscle-up, bodyweight-squat, incline-bench, back-squat or deadlift standard.** The brief's five tests remain covered only by the Strength Level rows and the proxies above.
6. The Galpin Essentials re-cuts (2026-09-17, 2026-09-24, 2026-04-02) are Premium-gated; the 2026-09-17 page was opened (S4) and confirms gating. Treat any claim of "revised" numbers in those episodes as UNVERIFIED.
7. Sources S5–S8 were full transcripts flagged "under human review and may contain errors."
