### A. Dr. Andy Galpin, "How to Assess & Improve All Aspects of Your Fitness" (S1, 2023-01-18)

1. **Nine adaptations framework.** Chapter 00:05:40 "9 Exercise-Induced Adaptations" (≈00:06:48–00:09:05). Galpin: skill, speed, power, strength, hypertrophy, muscular endurance ("typically in 5 to 50 repetition sort of range... almost always local muscle"), anaerobic capacity ("maximum amount of work you can do in, say, 30 to 45 seconds, maybe even up to 120 seconds"), maximal aerobic capacity ("8-to-15-minute range... true VO2 max"), long duration. Opinion/framework. Mapping: general; muscular endurance bin covers muscle-ups/bodyweight squats; long duration covers swimming.

2. **Grip strength (hand dynamometer).** Chapter 01:18:42 "Strength Test" (≈01:19:26–01:20:08). Galpin: "you want to look for something like a minimum score here of 40 kilograms. Ideally, you're up past 60, would be a really good spot to be in... If you are a male and you are under 40 kilograms on a hand grip dynamometer, we're going to need to train that. If you're a female... about 35 kilograms is the cutoff point. If you're above 55, we can add it to your training, but I'm not worried about leaving it out of your training." Opinion (no citation given; not body-weight-scaled). Mapping: general fitness proxy; relevant to muscle-up/pull hang. Tier map: <40 kg below floor; 40–55 kg = Above average; >60 kg = Strong & athletic; Elite not stated.

3. **Dead hang.** Chapter 01:18:42 (≈01:20:25–01:21:29). Galpin: "we should be able to hang for a minimum of 30 seconds... 30 to kind of 50 seconds is my good, but we could probably get better here. If you're cruising above 60 seconds, I'm generally pretty happy." He adds the body-mass caveat: "if you're 145 pounds, even if you're unhealthy, you're going to be able to hang for a long time. It's just not that much weight to carry. So just rough numbers." Opinion. Mapping: muscle-up (grip/hang prerequisite). For a 57 kg (126 lb) subject Galpin's own caveat implies these are floor values; use the upper end.

4. **Broad jump = your height.** Chapter 01:12:38 "Speed Test, Power Test" (≈01:13:05–01:13:21). Galpin: "So the cost-free version here is a simple broad jump... A super basic number to look for there is your height. So you should be able to broad jump how tall you are. If you're 5'5", you should hit 5'5", 6'5", et cetera." Opinion. Subject: 1.70 m. Mapping: general (power), not one of the five.

5. **Vertical jump ≥24 in.** Chapter 01:12:38 (≈01:15:01–01:16:45). Galpin (two-hand touch, countermovement allowed, no run-up): "You want to look for a number of something like 24 inches or higher. If you're past the age of 50, that number can come down a little bit to closer to 20. And again, for females, it's going to be ratcheted down about 15%." Opinion. Subject: ≥61 cm (24 in). Mapping: general (power).

6. **Upper-body 1RM: bench press acceptable but not his focus.** Chapter 01:18:42 (≈01:21:42). Galpin: "You can do an upper body strength test if you would like, although it's not technically something we do very often. Happy to do a one-rep max bench press or something like that." No number given here. Later, chapter 01:44:00 "Fitness Testing Frequency & Testing Order" (≈01:44:56): "if you can bench press double your body weight, I don't need to test your bench very often for the average person. If you're not a powerlifter, maybe once a year." Opinion; 2×BW is offered as a "so strong we stop testing" marker, not a health target. Subject: 2 × 57 = **114 kg flat bench**. Mapping: **incline bench proxy** (flat bench; no incline number exists anywhere in the catalogue).

7. **Lower-body strength: leg extension 1×BW for 1 rep (age <40).** Chapter 01:18:42 (≈01:22:02–01:23:21). Galpin: "for the average person, a leg extension test is fairly standardized... a very simple answer here is body weight. Can you do a leg extension with your body weight?" Huberman: "One repetition?" Galpin: "One repetition... If you go up in age past age 40, every decade, that can come down about 10%... So if you're 50 years old and you're 170 pounds, if you can do 160, you're in a pretty good sort of spot... prior to 40, there's really no change in strength." He explicitly prefers the back squat but calls it "very technically demanding". Opinion. Subject: **57 kg bilateral leg extension × 1**. Mapping: **back squat proxy**.

8. **1RM estimation rule.** Chapter 01:18:42 (≈01:23:47). Galpin: "put on a load that you think is kind of close to your maximum and just do it for as many reps as you can. As long as it's under five reps total, you can then actually go online and enter that into any number of calculators... If you get past five repetitions or so, the accuracy of those equations starts going down." Also: "the NSCA... has specific guides for exactly what to do for your warmup protocol prior to one-rep max testing." Opinion referencing NSCA. Mapping: applies to incline bench, squat, deadlift testing method.

9. **Goblet/front-squat bottom hold.** Chapter 01:18:42 (≈01:24:45–01:25:44). Galpin: "hold a weight in front of your chest... about half of your body weight, go all the way to the bottom position, and try to hold that for about 45 seconds... as long as you can hold that good position without a technical breakdown in that 45 seconds, that's a really good spot. As an intro, I want a third of your body weight for 30 seconds." Opinion. Subject: **28.5 kg × 45 s ("really good"); 19 kg × 30 s (intro)**. Mapping: **back squat / bodyweight squat proxy** (position + core).

10. **Muscular endurance: plank and push-ups.** Chapter 01:29:38 "Muscular Endurance Test, Push-Up" (≈01:30:00–01:32:28). Galpin: "can you hold a front plank for 60 seconds? Can you hold a side plank for 45 seconds?... for a general male, we should have no problem doing 25-plus consecutive pushups... 25 pushups for a male is a standard, and even something like 10 is a number we're looking for, again, as kind of minimum." Form: "full, complete lock out of the elbows on the top and a full chest touch or close to it at the ground"; "any break, failed test." Opinion. Mapping: incline-bench muscular-endurance proxy; general.

11. **Muscular endurance on the strength lift: 75% of 1RM for >8 reps.** Chapter 01:36:23 "Anaerobic Capacity Test, Heart Rate" (≈01:36:24–01:36:49). Galpin: "if you were able to do 200 pounds on your leg extension test, put 75% on that and do that as many reps as you can. You want to look for more than eight repetitions... a good eight to 12 sort of number." Opinion. Subject (leg extension 1RM = 57 kg): ≈43 kg × 8–12 reps. Mapping: squat-family muscular endurance.

12. **Anaerobic capacity: no standards; heart-rate recovery 30 bpm in 60 s.** Chapter 01:36:23 (≈01:36:59–01:39:29). Galpin: "Wingate test... 30-second maximal test"; "We don't really have standards for these things because it's going to be different"; recovery: "Within 60 seconds, you should have, again, that half a beat per second. You should have a heart rate recovery of 30 beats per minute." Opinion. Mapping: general; a swim-interval readiness marker.

13. **VO2max test options.** Chapter 01:39:29 "Maximal Heart Rate Test, VO2 Max" (≈01:40:07–01:42:11). Galpin: "12-minute Cooper's test... run for 12 minutes as far as you can... that's anywhere between a mile to two-plus miles, depending on how fit you are"; one-mile walk test with heart rate; "there's just a lot more science on the 12-minute Cooper test." Opinion pointing to standard field tests. Subject: 1.6–3.2+ km in 12 min is the range he quotes, no tiers. Mapping: swimming-endurance proxy (aerobic capacity).

14. **VO2max targets.** Chapter 01:52:44 "VO2 Max Measurements" (≈01:53:00–01:57:24). Galpin: "for men, a minimum number we want to look at here is 35 milliliters per kilogram per minute, and for women, that'd be about 30... In reality, I want to see men above 50... If I want to see men above 55, I really want to see women above 50 as the target... really high-level endurance folks. They may pass 70 or 80... football or basketball... 55, 65 sort of range. So if you, as an average person, are 55, that's a really good marker to be in." Also chapter 00:17:20 (≈00:20:27–00:21:28): "18 is what we call the line of independence"; "35 to 38 milliliters per kilogram per minute... about the VO2 max you would find for a normal college male" (data: his Swedish octogenarian cross-country skier study, described but not cited by DOI). Mixed data/opinion. Mapping: **swimming endurance** (aerobic ceiling). Tier map: 35 = floor; >50 = Above average; 55 = Strong & athletic; 70–80 = Elite.

15. **Long-duration steady state.** Chapter 01:42:42 "Long Duration Steady State Exercise Test" (≈01:43:10–01:43:57). Galpin: "you should maintain consistent work output for over 20-plus minutes... can you do this with nasal breathing only? That's when I feel really good. If you can go 30 straight minutes without needing to take a break... I want you moving at a non-walking pace... minimum of 20 minutes of work with no breaks." Opinion. Mapping: **swimming endurance** (continuous swim ≥20–30 min).

16. **Movement/skill screen.** Chapter 01:07:18 "Movement/Skill Test" (≈01:08:41–01:10:01). Galpin: test "an upper body press, an upper body pull, a lower body press, and a lower body pull. An example would be a pushup, a pull-up or a bent row, a squat, and then a deadlift... Slow and controlled... pause at the bottom, maybe three seconds, maybe five seconds or 10." Opinion. Mapping: all five (form check).

### C. Dr. Peter Attia

20. **Strength / VO2max mortality data.** S8 (2022-08-15), chapter 00:38:31 "All-Cause Mortality: Smoking, Strength, VO2 max" (≈00:38:47–00:40:16). Attia: "low muscle mass people have about a 3X hazard ratio... strength could be probably 3.5X as a hazard ratio... bottom 25% for their age and sex in terms of VO2 max... compare them to the people that are just at the 50th to 75th percentile, you're talking about a 2X difference roughly in the risk of ACM. Until your VO2 max is at least to the 75th percentile, and you're able to dead hang for at least a minute, and you're able to wall sit for at least two." **Important:** when Huberman later returns to the deadlift number (chapter 00:44:43, ≈00:44:59) Attia says "I just made that one up. 22. **Strength Metrics Assessment (SMA) numbers.** S8, chapter 00:38:31 into 00:44:43 "Attia's Rule of Supplementation, 'Centenarian Decathlete' Physical Goals" (≈00:43:20–00:45:28). We don't use a wall sit. We do just a straight squat, air squat, at 90 degrees, and I believe two minutes is the standard for both men and women at 40... for a male, you should be able to farmer carry your body weight for, I think we have two minutes... So that's half your body weight in each hand... For women, I think we're doing 75% of body weight." Opinion (clinic standards). Subject: dead hang 120 s; air-squat hold 120 s; farmer carry **28.5 kg per hand × 2 min**. Mapping: muscle-up (hang), bodyweight squat (hold), deadlift/grip (carry).

23. **Mile-time proxy declined.** S8 (≈00:44:10). Huberman asks "run a mile at seven minutes or less, eight minutes or less?"; Attia: "there are really good VO2 max estimators online... 24. **Back-casting example.** S8, chapter 00:32:16 (≈00:32:20). Attia: a demanding 90-year-old life "will require a VO2 max of 30 milliliters of oxygen per minute per kilogram... that declines at about 8% to 10% per decade." Opinion with physiological rule of thumb. Mapping: general.

25. **Power tests.** S9 (2023-03-20), chapter 02:31:20 "Fall Risk & Stability, 4 Pillars of Strength Training" (≈02:34:55–02:37:54). Attia: "When I was 18 years old, I could vertical jump over 30 inches. Subject: broad jump 1.83 m as an "arbitrary" adult-male floor; vertical jump 61 cm at 50, 76 cm at 18. Mapping: general (power).

26. **S10 (2024-07-29)** contains no standard; only "my vertical jump is literally half what it was when I was a teenager... one of the purest tests of power" (chapter 01:58:12, ≈02:01:25). Opinion.

## Caveats

1. Every number is a coach's or physician's stated opinion on a podcast, not a percentile from a normative dataset. Where a guest referenced data (Attia's VO2max hazard ratios; Galpin's Swedish skier study), the underlying paper was not named on air and must be verified in the peer-reviewed domain.
2. Timestamps: hubermanlab.com gives chapter-level times only; the "≈" offsets are position-based estimates within the cited chapter, not transcript-encoded times. YouTube auto-captions were not used (no browser automation permitted).
3. The S1 page states its transcript is "currently under human review and may contain errors"; quotes were copied verbatim from that text.
4. Galpin's grip (40/55/60 kg) and dead-hang numbers are not body-weight scaled; he explicitly notes a ~145 lb person hangs longer. The 57 kg subject should expect to exceed the hang floors and may find absolute grip targets relatively harder.
5. Attia's tiers are clinic goals for a 40-year-old; he says they are "adjusted up and down based on age," so a 28-year-old should meet or exceed them. His deadlift BW×10 was explicitly retracted as improvised.
6. Pavel's 2×BW Zercher is framed for "an athlete", not the general public, and his pull-up figure invokes the USMC standard second-hand.
7. Publication dates differ from the brief: Starrett aired 2024-12-09 (not 2023); Norton's first episode 2022-11-07 (not 2023); a second Norton (2024-08-12) and a second Cavaliere (2026-05-25) episode were also opened.
8. Kelly Starrett's Built to Move 10-point sit-rise scoring, step-count and balance vital-sign thresholds are in the book, not in the transcript; only the pass/fail sit-rise and couch-stretch tests were stated on air.
9. "Simple & Sinister" numeric standards were not stated in the Pavel episode; only the book/course was named.
10. Two Attia Journal Club episodes and the "Essentials" re-cuts were not opened; by title they contain no fitness benchmarks.
