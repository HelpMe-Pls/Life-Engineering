8. **Ball & Weidman 2018 (source #10, abstract).** 47,913 USAPL results, 2012–2016; "peak age of lifting power is between ages 24–49"; "a lighter person can generally lift a greater percentage of their weight than a heavier person"; raw totals significantly lower than equipped. Per-weight-class means for 53/59/66 kg are in the full text (HTTP 402) — **not obtained**; rows marked UNVERIFIED.

9. **Jaric 2002 (source #11, abstract, verbatim).** "The allometric parameter should be either b = 0.67 for muscle force (recorded by a dynamometer), or b = 1 for muscle torque." Worked example (b = 0.67): an 80 kg man squatting 2.0 ×BW = 160 kg is allometrically matched by a 57 kg man lifting 160 × (57/80)^0.67 = **127.5 kg = 2.24 ×BW**. General rule at 57 vs 80 kg: multiply the 80-kg lifter's load by **0.797**, or his ×BW ratio by **1.118**. So the folk targets "1.5 / 2 / 2.5 ×BW bench / squat / deadlift" (an 80-kg-centred rule of thumb) become **1.68 / 2.24 / 2.80 ×BW** = **96 / 127 / 159 kg** at 57 kg — almost exactly Strength Level Advanced-to-Elite. Cleather 2006 not opened (Gap).

10. **Solberg 2019 (source #12, abstract, verbatim).** World-class powerlifters: "Peak age (mean [SD]) was 35 (7) y for powerlifters and 26 (3) y for weightlifters"; five-year pre-peak improvement 12 % for powerlifters. Implication: at 28 the subject is ~7 years before the elite powerlifting peak age, so no age discount applies to any standard in this note; the brief's "for his age" is satisfied by the 20–29 / Open columns.

11. **IPF Technical Rules Book 2026, "immaculate form" definitions (source #13, verbatim).** Squat: "the lifter must bend the knees and lower the body until the top surface of the legs at the hip joint is lower than the top of the knees. Only one decent attempt is allowed … The lifter must recover at will to an upright position with the knees locked. Double bouncing at the bottom of the squat attempt or any downward movement is not permitted." Bench: "After receiving the signal, the lifter must lower the bar to the chest or abdominal area whereby the underside of both elbow joints is lowered level with or below the top surface of each respective shoulder joint (the bar shall not touch the belt), hold …" [continuation of the pause sentence not captured by extraction]; disqualifications include "Heaving, or sinking the bar after it has been motionless on the chest or abdominal area", "Any downward movement of the whole of the bar in the course of being pressed out", and "Failure to press the bar to straight arm's length elbows locked at the completion of the lift." Deadlift: "The Chief Referee's signal shall consist of a downward movement of the arm and the audible command 'Down'. The signal will not be given until the bar is held motionless and the lifter is in the apparent finished position." Causes for disqualification: "Any downward movement of the bar before it reaches the final position. Failure to stand erect with the shoulders back. Failure to lock the knees straight at the completion of the lift. Supporting the bar on the thighs during the performance of the lift." All numbers in the standards table assume these criteria (competition depth, paused bench, no hitching).

12. **Reconciliation.** For each lift the four families agree to within ~10 % once population is accounted for: Strength Level Intermediate ≈ Kilgore Intermediate (UNVERIFIED) ≈ Cooper 60th–80th; Strength Level Advanced ≈ Cooper 95th ≈ Kilgore Advanced ≈ British M1/University QT (total) ≈ DOTS 315–350; Strength Level Elite ≈ Kilgore Elite ≈ British U23 QT ≈ DOTS 400 / GL 75. GL 100 (593 kg total) is IPF world-class and is far above the brief's Elite tier.

## Caveats

- The three Kilgore/exrx pages were never read (403; Wayback blocked). Every Kilgore number above is agent recall and must be treated as UNVERIFIED; the standards table does not depend on them (Strength Level, Cooper, British Powerlifting and the formulas carry each row).
- Strength Level values are self-reported, self-selected lifters; a 57 kg man is at the thin lower tail of its bodyweight distribution, so the 55/60 kg rows rest on fewer entries than mid-weight rows.
- The Cooper table on #5 names the Cooper Institute but not an ACSM edition or table number; the 70th and 90th percentile rows requested in the brief do not exist on that page, so the Above-average and Strong & athletic ACSM anchors are the 80th row and an interpolation respectively.
- DOTS coefficients were taken from a search-engine summary of a GitHub repo (#9), not from an opened page. The IPF GL coefficients (#8) are from the official PDF and verbatim.
- British Powerlifting "Open" totals are set by a separate procedure not on the page; the Elite anchor uses U23 (452.5), which for a 28-year-old is a proxy, not his division.
- IPF bench rule: the sentence after "hold" (the motionless-on-chest requirement before the "Press" command) was cut by the PDF text extraction; the disqualification wording quoted in finding 11 establishes the same requirement.
- The IPF rulebook was read from a Latvian federation mirror dated "Updated 1 January 2026"; the official powerlifting.sport v3 (effective 1 March 2026) timed out and may differ in wording.
- "Elite" here = top ~1–5 % of trained lifters / national-qualifying, not IPF world-class (GL 100 = 593 kg total, about 30 % above the Elite row).
