The job Sarah can't win with a spreadsheet

A cert lapses quietly.
Then the inspector shows up.

"Mike's Electrical" is a 35-person contractor. By law, insurance, and client contracts, every electrician must hold valid certs. Sarah, the office manager, tracks all of it in a spreadsheet — until one date slips past her.

The chain reaction

One expired card, discovered at the wrong moment, doesn't cost you a reminder. It costs you the fine, the failed audit, the worker sent home, and sometimes the contract.

She tracks it by hand

Every employee × every cert × every expiry date — first-aid every 2 years, OSHA, state license, equipment cards — in one increasingly fragile spreadsheet.

2

A date slips past

No alarm goes off. A row that should be red just looks like every other row. The spreadsheet can't raise its hand.

3

The audit finds the gap

An inspector or a client audit lands before the renewal does → fine, failed audit, worker pulled, contract at risk.

4

Renewals are worse than reminders

Even when she catches it, she must find the training, chase the worker, collect the new PDF, and file the proof. A reminder alone leaves all four on her desk.

The real competitor isn't software — it's the spreadsheet. Free, trusted, already installed, and it never shuts down. Most of these are spreadsheet-replacement conversations, not software-comparison ones.
