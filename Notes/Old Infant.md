## The rungs and the counter for each

A rung fires when the last reply was late, ambiguous, or made him the reason. A timed ack at rung 1-2 and he never climbs. His threats are mood, not plans (nothing from rungs 3 and 6 was ever carried out): answer the order, never the threat.

1. **Exasperation** ("jesus. why so long?") → ack inside 5 min with a timebox: "On it, [what] by [time]."
2. **Blame** ("something about your X is wrong") → agree with the goal in his words, swap in your method: "Agreed, the checks should match the app. Cutting it down today, numbers by EOD."
3. **Unilateral order + replacement** ("delete them all, Khoa will do it") → let him win the words, keep the substance: do the essentials yourself today; name the other engineer only as reviewer, never rival.
4. **Chase on silence** ("hello?") → answer instantly. Never >5 min silent on a direct question. The ack goes out BEFORE consulting Claude (7 Sep: seven unanswered messages = the worst debit on record).
5. **Verdict + bypass** ("not acceptable, I'll do it myself") → release him ("Go ahead on your side, don't wait on me"), fix any breakage quietly, never "told you so".
6. **Institutionalize** (meeting "so we don't run into this going forward") → author the going-forward rule yourself in the thread, deliver before the meeting, add "fine to skip if this covers it".
7. **Reframe onto your competence** ("it's about your productivity… over engineered") → never refute, never sign. One-line ack of the principle at most, then delivery.

## His traps and the counters

Bait = an editorial with no question mark, a verdict word ("not acceptable", "waste of time", "over engineered"), the "it's about you" reframe, a name-drop of another engineer, a status challenge ("who do you think you are", "do you understand the scope?"), an authority assertion ("company discretion"), sarcasm, "please stop arguing with me".

- **Bait mixed with an order or question** → answer only the order or question, instantly, with a timebox.
- **Pure bait** → nothing, or one principle line, then deliver.
- **He states a wrong fact (in front of others)** → never correct him in the thread; fix it in the product, the training or the PR.
- **He sets a real constraint** (SOW gate, a date) → ride it, never argue it.
- **The client gates his push** (19 Nov: Nisha asked to see EO Vietnam on the sandbox before production) → ride it as the next step of his plan, never as EO slowing him; give him the date and the screenshots for the board.
- **"Not sure where the disconnect is"** → never explain; echo his priority ("Agreed, X is the only priority now") and give dates.
- **Usage or cost question** ("what are you coding that takes so much?") → what it does for the team plus the one big run; never the method, never "my own tasks".
- **Pressure from above** ("board", "contract in jeopardy") → echo his word ("blocker", "only priority"), never say whose job it is, never argue his premise, timebox a status.
- **He reassigns you** ("I'm going to move you off X") → yes to the direction, never to a start date in text; in the room tie the start to a date he already owns (the insurance record), one priority his to set, the new bar for "exceptional" in his words in the recap.
- **The hook test:** the urge to explain, prove you were right, or correct him = the trap. Cut those sentences. Never reply within 60 s of reading bait while angry.

## Levers

- Speed beats substance; re-time a self-set deadline before it passes.
- Hand him a principle to stand on; never make him the impatient one.
- Own a gap in one clause ("should have said that first"); no "sorry".
- Last message in a hot thread is a number or deliverable, then go dark.
- Never ask the same day as a blow-up; ask Mon-Wed, never Thu/Fri.
- Firm points only in a room with numbers on screen; in text, propose and defer.
- HR is his costume: never "is that HR's number or yours"; let him fix what the benchmark "missed".
- Every incident becomes evidence for the next raise ("You flagged it Thursday. It was fixed Thursday."), kept out of chat.
- Master filter for every line: does it GIVE or ASK? Asking shows appetite; appetite gets priced.

## Never

Apologize in writing; explain how a thing works; ask "why"; ask a question that invites him to restate an order; disparage the engineer he names; negotiate pay with a third person present; let pay drift into a work thread; push dates in text; book his calendar without his yes (propose a slot, he picks); tell EO in writing that Edge8's priorities are open; state a promise as done; invoice past help in chat; imperatives aimed at him ("name it…"); NET numbers (all gross).

## Voice and concealment

- Short declaratives, numbers over adjectives, a timebox in every commitment, one message per beat, no second message to fix the first.
- No emoji or "!" in a hot thread (Khoi adds his own in warm ones), no hedges (just, maybe, I think), no rhetorical questions.
- Concede words freely ("fair", "agreed"), never substance (the number, the scope, ownership). Get shorter when he is hot.
- Credit flows to him ("your call"); praise his judgment, never him as a person; never name a technique or quote him back with "as you said"; never win an argument out loud, win the paper. If he spots a technique: "Busted. I did my homework."
- When addressing him, write "your review", never "Dave's review".

**Chat template** (casual "yea", digits, no em dashes, ONE message, 2-4 sentences): [ack his goal in his words] + [what you are doing, by when] + [release or off-ramp] + [numbers by X].

## How Khoi wants lines written

Speaking under pressure is Khoi's weakest skill; spoken lines are read off-screen, so each must survive a nervous mouth.

**Chat lines:** reply text first; one message, 2-4 sentences, casual ("yea"), digits, no em dashes, no emoji, no greeting-card idioms or stock filler ("say the word", "I'm all ears"), no imperatives aimed at him, no announced patience ("these things take time"). Propose, never book.

**Spoken lines:** breath-sized, no em dashes, natural American.
- Praise his judgment, never grade his decisions or him as a person.
- No scorekeeping ("you met me, so I'm meeting you"), no self-justifying tag after a number, no invented shared history.
- No negation frames or minimizers in a number line ("I'm not asking for…", "just", "only"); prime the number as good ("sits comfortably inside your rule").
- No pressure words (today, close, call it); closes are no-oriented or his choice ("how does that sit with you?").
- Never ask him to reverse himself: hand him a new fact or a hypothetical, make his rule the hero, make the exception his to write, give him the clean answer for third parties.
- No weeds spoken (no phases, hours, dates); detail goes in the typed recap. No number inside a heat line.

**Working with Khoi on them:** preserve his hand-edits (diff before rewriting); never commit for him; for plan changes one grilling round with recommended answers, then wait for GO; flag honest gaps.
